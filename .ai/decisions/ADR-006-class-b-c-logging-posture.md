# ADR-006: Log credentials and personal data in full, inside an audited store

## Status

Accepted. Owner-signed: **Youssef Farag, 2026-09-20**. **Conditional — see below.**

## Context

A consumer-finance backend handling OTPs, tokens, national IDs and card data has to
choose what a request/response log may contain. Masking everything protects the log store
but makes per-request dispute resolution and incident forensics much harder.

## Decision

Two classes are logged **in full**, inside the audited request/response log store and
nowhere else:

- **Class B** — OTP, access and refresh tokens, `Authorization` header, session ID, API
  key, client secret, request-signature secret, biometric signature.
- **Class C** — national ID, passport, full phone, full name, DOB, address, email, bank
  account, IBAN, geolocation, device identifier, ID-document and liveness references,
  credit-bureau data, contact book.

**Class A is not covered by this decision.** PIN, password, PAN, CVV, card token and
private keys are masked always, in every environment, including while debugging — PCI DSS
prohibits storing CVV after authorization and requires PAN to be masked, and a documented
risk acceptance does not satisfy an assessor.

## The decision is conditional on all four remaining true

1. No API, dashboard, report or export exposes log contents.
2. Access to the log store is restricted; no general engineering or support access.
3. Retention is enforced at **90 days**, mechanically.
4. Logs are not replicated, restored or exported into a lower-trust environment.

**A change that breaks any of the four is a change to this decision**, not a feature. It
must be raised with the owner before it ships, and an agent that encounters one must stop
and ask.

## Alternatives Considered

| Option                                             | Pros                                                                      | Cons                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Log B and C in full under four conditions (chosen) | Complete per-request audit trail; disputes resolvable without reproducing | The log store becomes a credential and personal-data store, inheriting every obligation of one |
| Mask everything except Class D                     | Smallest blast radius                                                     | Loses the operational and dispute-resolution value the decision was taken for                  |
| Log B and C masked, with a break-glass unmask      | Middle ground                                                             | Needs a second access path and its own audit — more surface, not less                          |

## Consequences

- The log store **is** a credential and personal-data store. Access restriction and
  mechanical 90-day retention are load-bearing controls, not hygiene.
- **Residual risk, accepted knowingly:** tokens and OTPs expire, so a logged value is dead
  well before 90 days. Request-signature and API/client secrets do **not** expire — a
  logged secret stays valid until someone rotates it, and rotation is the only control
  bounding that one.
- Class B and C **MUST NOT** leave the audited store — not to stdout, an error tracker, an
  APM or any third-party service. This is why `no-console` is an `error` in the canonical
  lint config: `console` writes to stdout, outside the store and outside its retention.
- The document describing this is internal. `README.md` § "Before sharing externally"
  requires a stripped fork before any of it is shared.
